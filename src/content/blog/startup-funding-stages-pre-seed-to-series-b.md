*Pre-seed, seed, Series A, Series B: what each stage means, which instruments are common, and what to prepare before you raise.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Seed is usually the first formal round; Carta reports a median seed round of $3.5 million in 2024 and a typical range of about $500,000 to $5 million.
- Convertibles (SAFEs and notes) are common at seed; priced rounds sell preferred stock at a set price.
- Later rounds generally bring heavier paperwork and diligence.
- Update your cap table, sign IP assignments, model dilution, and involve counsel before you raise.

Every funding stage has its own investors, instruments, and paperwork. Knowing what comes next helps you prepare early.

![The funding ladder](/images/startup-funding-stages-pre-seed-to-series-b/ladder.webp)


## 1. The funding ladder

Startups usually raise money in stages. The labels (pre-seed, seed, Series A, Series B, and later) describe roughly how far along the company is and what kind of investor and paperwork you should expect.

![The funding ladder: pre-seed for idea to early product, seed for the first formal round, Series A as a priced round, Series B to scale what works, then later rounds for growth or exit](/images/startup-funding-stages-pre-seed-to-series-b/ladder.webp)

The stages are not rigid. Companies skip steps, add "seed extension" rounds, or raise from different investor types at the same stage.

## 2. Pre-seed and seed

Carta describes a seed round as usually a startup's first formal institutional round, used to validate the idea and build it into an operating business. Companies are typically ready when they have a product to demo, though they may still need an MVP or proof of concept.

On size, Carta says seed rounds typically range from about **$500,000 to $5 million**, and reports a **median seed round of $3.5 million in 2024**. Investors at this stage include angels, venture firms, friends and family, and accelerators.

Pre-seed money is generally earlier and smaller, often from founders' networks and accelerators. See our guide to [launching an AI startup in the U.S.](/blog/how-to-launch-ai-startup-us/).

## 3. Series A and beyond

A **Series A** is commonly the first **priced round**, where investors buy preferred stock at a set price per share. Later rounds (Series B and beyond) generally fund scaling what is working, and the paperwork is typically heavier, with more investor rights and diligence at each step.

Because round sizes and valuations shift from year to year and source to source, check current benchmarks from a source such as Carta before you anchor on a number. We have not included Series A or B dollar figures here for that reason.

## 4. Three common instruments

![Three common instruments: a SAFE is equity later and not debt, a convertible note is debt that converts, and a priced round sells stock at a set price](/images/startup-funding-stages-pre-seed-to-series-b/instruments.webp)

Per Carta: a **SAFE** is equity, not debt, that converts into stock in a later priced round, and it can be post-money or pre-money, which affects investor dilution. A **convertible note** is debt that converts into equity at a future event such as a priced round. A **priced round** sells preferred stock at a set price, and preferred stock can carry rights such as liquidation preference. Carta also says convertibles are most common at seed because they are usually faster and simpler.

Go deeper: [SAFE vs. convertible note](/blog/safe-vs-convertible-note-startups/), [reviewing a SAFE before you sign](/blog/safe-review-before-you-sign/), and [NVCA documents for a priced round](/blog/nvca-model-documents-priced-round-explained/).

## 5. Seven things to do before you raise

![Seven things to do before you raise: update the cap table, build the pitch deck, sign IP assignments, organize company records, model dilution, pick your instrument, and find legal counsel](/images/startup-funding-stages-pre-seed-to-series-b/before-raise.webp)

Carta's process steps include preparing a pitch deck and updating the cap table, modeling dilution for convertible versus priced structures, and involving legal counsel when you receive a term sheet. ## 6. Where AI helps and where it stops

AI is useful for explaining terms, preparing a question list, and comparing scenarios. It should not be your only check on financing documents, because the terms affect ownership for years. Verify numbers and terms against the documents yourself; see [verifying AI-generated legal documents](/blog/verify-ai-generated-legal-document/).
