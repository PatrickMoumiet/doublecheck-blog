*If your startup is a Delaware corporation, you owe an annual franchise tax. Here are the deadlines, the two calculation methods, and a yearly routine.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Delaware corporations file an annual report and pay franchise tax online by March 1.
- There are two methods: Authorized Shares (minimum $175) and Assumed Par Value Capital (minimum $400).
- The maximum is $200,000, or $250,000 for Large Corporate Filers.
- Late filing carries a $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest. Run both methods and use the lower tax.

Forming a Delaware corporation is the beginning, not the end. The franchise tax comes back every year.

![Delaware franchise tax numbers to know](/images/delaware-franchise-tax-startups/key-numbers.webp)


## 1. What the Delaware franchise tax is

Many startups incorporate in Delaware. Every Delaware corporation has to file an annual report and pay an annual franchise tax. It is a yearly cost of being a Delaware corporation, separate from the one-time cost of forming it.

According to the Delaware Division of Corporations, domestic corporation annual reports and franchise taxes for the prior year are due **on or before March 1** and must be filed online.

## 2. Two methods, two minimums

Delaware lets corporations calculate the tax in two ways:

![Two ways to calculate Delaware franchise tax: the Authorized Shares Method has a $175 minimum and the Assumed Par Value Capital Method has a $400 minimum, each with a $200,000 maximum](/images/delaware-franchise-tax-startups/two-methods.webp)

- **Authorized Shares Method:** minimum tax of **$175**.
- **Assumed Par Value Capital Method:** minimum tax of **$400**.
- **Maximum:** $200,000 under both methods. Corporations identified as Large Corporate Filers have a maximum of $250,000.

The calculation details sit on separate Delaware pages and in Delaware's franchise tax calculator, so use the official calculator for your own numbers.

## 3. Why startups should check both methods

Startups often create a large number of authorized shares to leave room for founders, employees, and investors, while holding very little in assets. Depending on those numbers, the two methods can produce very different results, so run both and file using the one that gives the lower tax. If your first bill looks surprisingly high, that is usually the reason to check the other method before paying.

![The numbers to know: due March 1, $175 and $400 minimums, $200,000 maximum, $250,000 for large corporate filers, and a late charge of $200 plus 1.5% monthly interest](/images/delaware-franchise-tax-startups/key-numbers.webp)

## 4. What happens if you file late

Delaware's page states a late filing penalty of **$200 plus 1.5% monthly interest** on the tax and penalty. Calendar the March 1 date and assign someone to own it.

## 5. A simple yearly routine

![Yearly routine: gather your numbers, run both methods, pick the lower tax, file online, and pay by March 1](/images/delaware-franchise-tax-startups/yearly-steps.webp)

1. Gather your authorized shares, issued shares, and balance sheet numbers.
2. Run both methods using Delaware's calculator.
3. Use the method that produces the lower tax.
4. File the annual report and tax online.
5. Pay before March 1.

Ask your accountant or registered agent to confirm the filing, especially in your first year.

## 6. Does this affect LLCs?

This article covers corporations. Delaware LLCs have their own annual requirements, so check the Division of Corporations for the entity you actually formed. If you are weighing structures, see [LLC vs. C-corp](/blog/llc-vs-c-corp-startups/).
