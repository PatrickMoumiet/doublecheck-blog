*A comprehensive state regulatory guide to the Utah Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (Utah Code Title 13, Chapter 2 / SB 149), commercial disclosure mandates, consumer protection liabilities, and compliance as of November 2026. General information, not legal advice.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Enacted as a national pioneer on May 1, 2024, the **Utah Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (SB 149)** establishes clear commercial disclosure rules and strict liability standards for deploying generative AI.
- Businesses cannot escape liability under consumer protection laws; the statute explicitly establishes that **the "AI defense" is prohibited**.
- The law institutes a **tiered disclosure framework**, enforcing mandatory proactive disclosures for regulated professions (healthcare, legal) and on-demand disclosures for general commercial businesses.
- The **Utah Division of Consumer Protection** enforces compliance under the Consumer Sales Practices Act (UCSPA), levying administrative fines of up to **$2,500 per violation**.
- Utah balances consumer enforcement with an innovative **Regulatory Sandbox and AI Learning Laboratory**, allowing companies to test novel AI tools under mitigated state oversight.

## 1. The quick answer

While many states have grappled with sprawling, complex omnibus AI legislation, Utah enacted a targeted, consumer-protection-focused framework: **Senate Bill 149 (SB 149)**, codified as the **Artificial Intelligence Policy Act**.

Taking effect on May 1, 2024, and refined through subsequent legislative updates, Utah's law establishes two core rules for the commercial deployment of generative artificial intelligence:
1. **Transparency & Disclosures:** Businesses interacting with consumers through conversational AI must disclose that the consumer is communicating with an artificial intelligence system, with heightened proactive requirements for licensed professionals.
2. **Absolute Corporate Accountability:** A business cannot assert as a defense in any regulatory or civil enforcement proceeding that a deceptive act, false promise, or consumer law violation was committed by an autonomous AI model. The corporate deployer remains fully liable.

## 2. Overview and structure of Utah SB 149

The Utah Artificial Intelligence Policy Act was designed to safeguard Utah consumers from automated deception without strangling technological innovation.

The statute achieves this balance through three structural mechanisms:
- **Codified Deceptive Practice Definitions:** Integrating AI violations directly into the established **Utah Consumer Sales Practices Act (UCSPA)**, enabling rapid administrative enforcement by the Utah Division of Consumer Protection.
- **Tiered Disclosure Responsibilities:** Distinguishing between standard commercial interactions (such as retail e-commerce chatbots) and sensitive, high-stakes licensed professions.
- **The Office of AI Policy and Regulatory Sandbox:** Establishing an administrative office within the Utah Department of Commerce that operates an "AI Learning Laboratory," granting regulatory mitigation agreements to participating tech innovators.

## 3. Tiered disclosure requirements: General vs regulated

Utah's statute establishes clear, practical disclosure rules depending on the industry and the nature of the consumer interaction:

### Regulated Occupations (High-Stakes Disclosures)
For businesses or individuals providing services in occupations licensed by the Utah Department of Commerce (such as healthcare providers, psychologists, clinical therapists, and licensed legal service providers):
- **Proactive Upfront Disclosure:** The professional or practice must **proactively, clearly, and conspicuously disclose** to the consumer at the outset of the interaction that they are communicating with or receiving material generated by generative artificial intelligence.
- **Communication Channels:** Applies across text, audio, and visual interfaces. A consumer cannot be left guessing whether a medical summary or legal intake assessment was drafted by a human or a machine.

### General Commercial Businesses (On-Demand Disclosures)
For general commercial suppliers (such as retail websites, SaaS customer support bots, and automated reservation systems):
- **On-Demand Transparency:** The supplier must clearly disclose that the consumer is interacting with generative AI **when asked or prompted by the consumer**.
- **Prominent Attribution:** If an automated system provides customized sales advice or contractual terms, the consumer has the statutory right to unambiguous disclosure upon inquiry.

## 4. The statutory prohibition of the AI defense

The most significant legal innovation in Utah SB 149 is its absolute elimination of the "algorithmic excuse."

Codified in Utah Code § 13-2-11:
> *“It is not a defense to a violation of an act administered by the Division of Consumer Protection that generative artificial intelligence made the violative statement, undertook the violative act, or was used in furtherance of the violation.”*

This statutory bar is critical for enterprise risk management. If a commercial customer service bot promises an unauthorized refund, fabricates a warranty term, or engages in deceptive price representations, the business cannot defend itself by claiming that the large language model experienced a hallucination or acted outside its system prompt. Under Utah law, the model is the legal agent of the business deployer, and the business bears 100% of the legal liability.

## 5. Enforcement and administrative penalties

Enforcement of the Artificial Intelligence Policy Act is handled by the **Utah Division of Consumer Protection** and the **Utah Attorney General**:

- **Administrative Enforcement:** The Director of the Division of Consumer Protection can initiate administrative investigations, subpoena records, issue cease-and-desist orders, and seek consumer restitution.
- **Statutory Civil Penalties:** The Division may assess administrative fines of up to **$2,500 per violation**. Because fines can be calculated per individual consumer transaction or interaction, cumulative financial exposure escalates rapidly.
- **Intentional Order Violations:** If a business intentionally violates a formal cease-and-desist order or administrative consent decree, courts can levy civil penalties of up to **$5,000 per violation**, alongside court-ordered attorney's fees.

## 6. Business compliance and chatbot protocol

Companies operating consumer-facing artificial intelligence tools in Utah must implement a four-part compliance protocol:

1. **Configure Automated Conversational Disclosures:** Program all customer-facing chatbots and interactive voice response (IVR) systems to recognize user inquiries regarding human vs. AI status (e.g., "Are you a real person?"), responding instantly with unambiguous confirmation of AI identity.
2. **Implement Proactive Headers in Regulated Fields:** For telehealth, therapy, and legal tech applications, embed permanent, prominent visual badges stating: *"This service utilizes generative artificial intelligence under human professional supervision."*
3. **Conduct Prompt Engineering Audits for Deceptive Claims:** Review and test system prompts to ensure conversational AI tools do not make unsubstantiated product claims or unauthorized commercial commitments.
4. **Evaluate Regulatory Sandbox Participation:** For innovative tech startups developing complex, cutting-edge AI systems, consider applying to Utah's Office of AI Policy Learning Laboratory to test products under formal state regulatory mitigation agreements.

## FAQ

**What is the Utah Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (SB 149)?**

The Utah Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (Senate Bill 149) is a pioneering state statute enacted in 2024 that established commercial disclosure mandates for generative AI and prohibited businesses from blaming AI for deceptive practices.

**When must a general business disclose that a customer is interacting with AI in Utah?**

Under Utah law, general commercial suppliers must clearly and conspicuously disclose that a consumer is interacting with generative artificial intelligence when prompted or asked by the consumer.

**What are the stricter AI disclosure rules for regulated professions in Utah?**

Individuals or companies providing services in regulated occupations (such as healthcare, mental health therapy, and legal practice) must proactively disclose generative AI use at the start of the interaction.

**Can a business blame an AI chatbot for making a false or deceptive statement in Utah?**

No. The Utah statute explicitly states that it is not a defense that generative AI made the violative statement or undertook the unlawful act; the business deployer remains fully liable.

**What is the Utah AI Learning Laboratory and Regulatory Sandbox?**
