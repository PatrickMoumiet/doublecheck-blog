*A comprehensive securities regulatory guide to Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) Regulatory Notice 24-09, supervisory obligations under Rule 3110, customer communication rules under Rule 2210, and generative AI compliance as of November 2026. General information, not legal advice.*

**Key takeaways:**

- FINRA's official **Regulatory Notice 24-09** establishes that the authority's rulebook is **technology-neutral**, applying fully to Generative AI (GenAI) and Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Broker-dealers cannot disclaim regulatory responsibility; the **"AI did it" defense is invalid**, and firms remain strictly liable for supervisory failures under **FINRA Rule 3110**.
- Automated client-facing messaging, marketing, and market commentary generated by AI must comply with **FINRA Rule 2210**, mandating fair, balanced, and truthful communication without hallucinations.
- Under **FINRA Rule 4511 and SEC Rule 17a-4**, firms must maintain complete, immutable audit trails of all business-related AI prompts, responses, and model versioning.
- Member firms must enforce strict data security protocols to ensure customer Non-Public Personal Information (NPI) is never leaked into public model training sets in violation of **Regulation S-P**.

## 1. The quick answer

As generative artificial intelligence and autonomous software agents enter the financial services sector, broker-dealers are leveraging large language models to summarize equity research, draft customer emails, synthesize institutional filings, and streamline back-office compliance.

In **Regulatory Notice 24-09**, the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) delivered a definitive reminder: **FINRA rules apply to generative AI with identical force as to human registered representatives**. A member firm deploying artificial intelligence must maintain comprehensive Written Supervisory Procedures (WSPs), guarantee that automated communications meet strict standards of fairness and balance, and archive all algorithmic records in compliance with federal securities laws. Outsourcing functions to an AI vendor does not transfer supervisory accountability.

## 2. The technology-neutral regulatory mandate

FINRA’s fundamental regulatory premise is that securities laws do not differentiate based on the underlying technology deployed. Whether an investment recommendation or marketing piece is drafted by a junior registered representative or generated by a multi-billion parameter neural network, the statutory obligations remain constant.

In Notice 24-09, FINRA articulated member firm obligations across the core AI deployment lifecycle:
- **Model Evaluation and Vetting:** Before deploying any generative model, firms must understand the tool's architecture, data sources, known limitations, and susceptibility to hallucinations.
- **Continuous Monitoring:** Ongoing performance monitoring is required to detect model drift, algorithmic bias, or emerging compliance gaps over time.
- **Zero Regulatory Exemption:** Automation offers operational efficiency, but creates zero regulatory immunity. A flawed AI-generated trade confirmation or misleading marketing summary represents a direct rule violation.

## 3. Supervisory responsibilities under FINRA Rule 3110

Under **FINRA Rule 3110 (Supervision)**, each member firm must establish and maintain a supervisory system reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable securities laws and FINRA rules.

Applying Rule 3110 to generative AI requires firms to institute specialized internal controls:
- **Written Supervisory Procedures (WSPs):** Firms must update their WSPs to include dedicated sections governing the approval, deployment, and testing of generative AI applications.
- **Designation of Supervisory Principals:** Qualified registered principals must be assigned explicit responsibility for overseeing AI workflows and reviewing automated outputs.
- **Human-in-the-Loop Controls:** For high-risk use cases—such as customer portfolio reviews, investment recommendations, or regulatory filings—firms must mandate human principal review and sign-off before the AI output is finalized or transmitted.

If a broker-dealer permits registered representatives to use unauthorized consumer chatbots to draft correspondence with retail clients, the firm faces immediate sanctions for failure to supervise under Rule 3110.

## 4. Public communications and FINRA Rule 2210

**FINRA Rule 2210 (Communications with the Public)** sets strict ethical and transparency benchmarks for all member firm marketing, research reports, social media posts, and client correspondence.

Generative AI introduces substantial risks under Rule 2210:
- **Hallucinations and Factual Inaccuracies:** LLMs are known to invent facts, misstate corporate earnings, or fabricate historical market performance. Disseminating an AI hallucination to prospective investors violates Rule 2210’s requirement that communications be fair, balanced, and accurate.
- **Unbalanced Promotional Language:** Generative models frequently output persuasive, optimistic text that downplays investment risks or promises exaggerated returns, directly contravening the prohibition on promissory statements.
- **Pre-Use Approval Requirements:** Under Rule 2210(b), retail communications often require principal pre-use approval. Firms must establish automated gatekeeping software ensuring AI-generated client messaging cannot bypass registered principal review.

## 5. Books, records, and archiving under Rule 4511

Broker-dealers operate under some of the most rigorous recordkeeping mandates in the global economy. Under **FINRA Rule 4511** and **Securities Exchange Act (SEA) Rule 17a-4**, firms must make and preserve books and records reflecting all aspects of their securities business.

Applying these rules to AI systems creates complex technical archiving obligations:
1. **Prompt and Response Archival:** If an employee uses an enterprise AI tool to formulate trade strategies, draft client memos, or summarize research, the firm must archive both the initial user prompt and the exact output generated by the model.
2. **Model Versioning and Parameter Logs:** Because foundation models are frequently updated by software vendors, firms must log the specific model version, temperature setting, and system instructions in place when a specific output was generated.
3. **WORM Storage Compliance:** All archived AI communications and records must be stored in Write-Once-Read-Many (WORM) compliant electronic formats, readily accessible for FINRA and SEC regulatory examinations.

## 6. Broker-dealer AI compliance blueprint

Member firms seeking to harness generative AI while maintaining clean regulatory standing must execute a four-stage compliance roadmap:

1. **Establish an AI Steering & Governance Committee:** Create an oversight board uniting compliance, legal, trading, IT security, and risk management to review and approve all proposed AI deployments.
2. **Implement Enterprise Wrappers with Zero-Training Guarantees:** Block employee access to consumer-grade chatbots on firm devices, deploying enterprise AI environments contractually guaranteed not to train on firm data or retain sensitive financial records.
3. **Upgrade Supervision & Principal Approval Gateways:** Integrate automated compliance monitoring tools that flag, review, and archive AI-generated client communications before delivery.
4. **Conduct Regular Third-Party Vendor Audits:** Demand comprehensive technical documentation, SOC 2 Type II reports, and cybersecurity penetration testing from every third-party financial AI provider.

## FAQ

**What is FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09?**

FINRA Regulatory Notice 24-09 is official regulatory guidance reminding member broker-dealers that FINRA rules are technology-neutral and apply fully when deploying Generative AI and Large Language Models.

**Can a broker-dealer blame an AI tool for a supervisory failure under FINRA Rule 3110?**

No. FINRA explicitly states that the 'AI did it' defense is invalid. Member firms and their designated supervisors remain strictly accountable for all activities conducted by or through automated systems.

**How does FINRA Rule 2210 apply to AI-generated client communications?**

Under Rule 2210, all communications with the public must be fair, balanced, and not misleading. AI hallucinations or false statements generated in client emails, marketing decks, or chatbots constitute direct rule violations.

**What recordkeeping requirements apply to AI prompts and outputs under FINRA Rule 4511?**

Firms must retain records of all business communications and algorithmic decisions under FINRA Rule 4511 and SEC Rule 17a-4, meaning user prompts, model outputs, and parameter settings must be archived and easily retrievable.

**What risks does FINRA highlight regarding customer data privacy and AI?**

FINRA highlights the severe risk of exposing non-public personal information (NPI) to public AI training datasets in violation of Regulation S-P, mandating enterprise data isolation and strict vendor vetting.
