*Who owns what your team builds? An IP assignment agreement answers that, and investors will ask.*

**Key takeaways:**

- These agreements protect confidential information and make the company the owner of work created for it.
- Founders, employees, contractors, advisors, and interns may all need to sign.
- Wording matters: assign IP rather than only promising to.
- Get signatures before work starts and have counsel check state-law limits.

If a contractor wrote your core code and never signed anything, who owns it? Fix that question before an investor asks.

![What the agreement does](/images/confidentiality-ip-assignment-agreements-startups/what-it-covers.webp)


## 1. What these agreements do

A confidentiality and IP assignment agreement does two things. It keeps the company's confidential information protected, and it says the work a person creates for the company belongs to the company.

![What the agreement does: confidentiality protects secrets, IP assignment makes the company own the work, and investors ask for it in diligence](/images/confidentiality-ip-assignment-agreements-startups/what-it-covers.webp)

Investors look for exactly this. A Series A legal checklist from Allied VC lists proof that founders, employees, and contractors assigned their IP to the company; see our [legal due diligence checklist](/blog/series-a-legal-due-diligence-checklist/).

## 2. Who should sign one

![Who should sign: founders, employees, contractors, advisors who contribute work, interns, and earlier contributors](/images/confidentiality-ip-assignment-agreements-startups/who-signs.webp)

Founders, employees, and contractors are the core. Do not forget advisors who contribute work, interns, and anyone who helped before the company existed. Get signatures **before** the work starts where possible.

## 3. Assign, don't just promise

The wording matters. An agreement that only promises to assign IP in the future is weaker than one that assigns it. Past work and pre-incorporation work should be covered too. Have counsel check the language for your state.

![Seven checks before you use one: assigns rather than promises, covers past work, clear scope, exclusions listed, signed and dated, state law limits, counsel reviewed](/images/confidentiality-ip-assignment-agreements-startups/checks.webp)

## FAQ

**What is an IP assignment agreement?**

An agreement under which a person transfers ownership of work they create for the company to the company.

**Who should sign one?**

Founders, employees, and contractors, and potentially advisors, interns, and earlier contributors.

**Why do investors ask for IP assignments?**

They want proof that the company owns the intellectual property it relies on.

**Can I use an AI-drafted version?**

As a first draft, yes. Verify it for your state and get counsel review before rolling it out.
