*A comprehensive state compliance guide to the Illinois Human Rights Act (IHRA) amendments under House Bill 3773 (Public Act 103-0801), algorithmic discrimination liability, mandatory employee notices, and HR compliance as of November 2026. General information, not legal advice.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Effective **January 1, 2026**, Illinois House Bill 3773 amends the Illinois Human Rights Act (IHRA) to make it an unlawful civil rights violation to use artificial intelligence that results in discriminatory disparate impact.
- The law covers the **entire employment lifecycle**, including recruitment, hiring, promotion, job renewal, training, discharge, discipline, and compensation.
- Employers are legally mandated to provide **formal written notice** to job applicants and employees before artificial intelligence is utilized in employment-related decisions.
- HB 3773 specifically outlaws the use of **postal ZIP codes or geographic proxies** in automated decision-making systems.
- Multi-state employers hiring remote workers located in Illinois are subject to full IDHR enforcement, administrative penalties, and civil damages.

## 1. The quick answer

Illinois has cemented its position as one of the nation's most aggressive regulators of workplace technology. Following its pioneering 2020 Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (AIVIA), Illinois enacted **House Bill 3773 (Public Act 103-0801)**, significantly expanding the **Illinois Human Rights Act (IHRA)**.

Taking effect on **January 1, 2026**, HB 3773 prohibits employers, employment agencies, and labor organizations from using artificial intelligence that has the effect of discriminating against protected classes. The law does not require proof of discriminatory intent; a disparate impact created by an algorithm constitutes an actionable civil rights violation. Furthermore, employers must provide upfront written notice to job applicants and employees whenever AI tools are deployed.

## 2. The evolution of Illinois AI employment law

Illinois's regulatory approach to workplace AI has developed through consecutive legislative actions:

1. **The Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (AIVIA) (820 ILCS 42/):** Enacted in 2020, AIVIA forced employers using AI to analyze recorded video interviews to notify applicants, explain how the technology works, obtain advance consent, and restrict video sharing.
2. **House Bill 3773 (Public Act 103-0801):** Recognizing that AI adoption had expanded far beyond video interviews into algorithmic resume sorting, automated background filtering, and predictive promotion scoring, the legislature amended the IHRA directly.
3. **The Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act (SB 315):** Signed in mid-2026, targeting frontier foundational AI developers with mandatory third-party audits and risk reporting frameworks.

Together, these statutes create a comprehensive compliance framework for businesses operating or hiring in Illinois.

## 3. Core statutory provisions of HB 3773

HB 3773 amends Section 2-102 of the Illinois Human Rights Act, establishing that it is a civil rights violation:

> *“For an employer to use artificial intelligence that has the effect of subjecting employees to discrimination on the basis of any protected class... or to use zip codes as a proxy for protected classes.”*

The statute defines artificial intelligence broadly as:
> *“An engineered or machine-based system that can, for a given set of objectives, generate outputs such as predictions, recommendations, or decisions influencing real or virtual environments.”*

### Broad Employment Scope
Unlike municipal laws that restrict oversight to initial hiring, HB 3773 governs automated systems used for:
- Recruitment and candidate sourcing
- Hiring and applicant shortlisting
- Promotion and leadership succession tracking
- Discipline, write-ups, and performance coaching
- Layoffs, reductions in force (RIFs), and terminations
- Compensation structuring and fringe benefits

## 4. The explicit ban on ZIP code proxies

A groundbreaking feature of HB 3773 is its **express statutory ban on using ZIP codes as a proxy for protected classes**.

In algorithmic modeling, machine learning engines frequently identify geographic postal codes as predictive variables for candidate commute times, tenure likelihood, or salary requirements. However, due to well-documented historical patterns of residential segregation, postal ZIP codes closely correlate with racial and ethnic demographics.

The Illinois legislature eliminated ambiguity by codifying that using ZIP codes—or algorithmic features derived from ZIP codes—to filter, score, or disqualify applicants constitutes an unlawful civil rights practice. Employers must audit their hiring models to ensure geographic filtering does not serve as an impermissible demographic proxy.

## 5. Mandatory employer notice obligations

In addition to substantive anti-discrimination mandates, HB 3773 enacts a strict **transparency and disclosure requirement**:

- **Timing of Notice:** Employers must notify applicants and current employees whenever artificial intelligence is utilized in selection, evaluation, or employment status decisions.
- **Form of Disclosure:** Notices must be clear, conspicuous, and accessible, detailing that automated machine-based systems are active in the evaluation process.
- **Rulemaking Authority:** The Illinois Department of Human Rights (IDHR) is tasked with adopting administrative rules detailing the specific formatting, content, and delivery timing required for compliant disclosures.

Failing to provide statutory notice constitutes an independent violation of the IHRA, exposing employers to administrative charges even in the absence of demonstrable disparate impact.

## 6. Employer risk management and audit protocol

To prepare for and comply with Illinois HB 3773, corporate legal teams and HR departments must execute a comprehensive compliance review:

1. **Catalog All Algorithmic Decision Systems:** Map every software application, ATS plugin, and third-party SaaS tool that ranks, screens, or categorizes candidates or employees.
2. **Eliminate Prohibited Data Inputs:** Inspect software parameters to ensure that postal codes, geographic distance filters, and proxy variables are purged from candidate screening algorithms.
3. **Publish Compliant Candidate Notices:** Draft and embed standardized AI disclosure statements into online job portals, application acknowledgment emails, and employee performance management handbooks.
4. **Conduct Pre-Deployment Bias Testing:** Partner with third-party testing firms to calculate demographic impact ratios and verify that candidate pass rates do not trigger disparate impact liability under the IHRA.
5. **Secure Vendor Compliance Warranties:** Update vendor contracts to mandate that HR technology providers warrant compliance with Illinois law and provide underlying validation documentation.

## FAQ

**What is Illinois House Bill 3773 (HB 3773)?**

HB 3773 is landmark legislation that amends the Illinois Human Rights Act (IHRA) effective January 1, 2026, making it an unlawful civil rights violation for employers to use AI systems that result in discriminatory disparate impact.

**Does Illinois HB 3773 require employers to notify job applicants about AI?**

Yes. Employers are legally mandated to notify job applicants and existing employees whenever artificial intelligence is utilized in recruitment, hiring, promotion, discipline, or discharge decisions.

**Why does Illinois HB 3773 explicitly ban the use of ZIP codes in AI?**

The statute specifically prohibits using ZIP codes as a proxy for protected classes, recognizing that historical residential segregation makes postal codes a frequent algorithmic surrogate for race and national origin.

**How does HB 3773 interact with the Illinois AI Video Interview Act (AIVIA)?**

HB 3773 expands upon AIVIA. While AIVIA specifically regulated video interview analysis, HB 3773 covers all AI tools across the entire employment lifecycle, from resume scrapers to promotion ranking software.

**Can multi-state employers hiring remote workers in Illinois ignore HB 3773?**

No. If an employer recruits, hires, or employs workers residing in Illinois, the employer must comply with the Illinois Human Rights Act, including HB 3773 notice and anti-discrimination mandates.
