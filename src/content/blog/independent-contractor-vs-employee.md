*Calling someone a contractor does not make them one. Here is what to look at before you hire.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Classification depends on how the work actually happens, and tests differ by state and by law.
- Common signals include control, schedule, tools, other clients, duration, and integration.
- A contract label helps, but it does not override reality.
- Write a fitting agreement, operate consistently, and get advice if unsure.

Worker classification is one of those topics where a small shortcut can become a large problem.

![Contractor or employee: six signals](/images/independent-contractor-vs-employee/signals.webp)


## 1. Why classification matters

Calling someone an independent contractor does not make them one. Agencies and courts look at how the relationship actually works, and the tests vary by state and by the law involved (wage, tax, benefits, or unemployment).

Getting it wrong can lead to back pay, tax, and penalty questions. That is why this belongs on your early checklist when you hire help.

## 2. Six signals to look at

These factors commonly come up, though no single one decides:

![Contractor or employee, six signals: control, schedule, tools, other clients, duration, and integration into core operations](/images/independent-contractor-vs-employee/signals.webp)

- **Control:** who directs how the work is done?
- **Schedule:** who sets the hours?
- **Tools:** who supplies equipment and software?
- **Other clients:** can the person work for others?
- **Duration:** a defined project or ongoing work?
- **Integration:** is the work central to your daily operations?

## 3. The label is not the test

A contract that says "independent contractor" helps document your intent, but it will not override reality.

![The label is not the test: the contract says independent contractor, but what can matter is actual control, actual independence, and the applicable state test](/images/independent-contractor-vs-employee/label-vs-reality.webp)

Some states, such as California, use stricter tests than others. Check the rules where the worker is located.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between an independent contractor and an employee?**

Generally, a contractor runs their own work with more independence, while an employee works under the employer's control. The exact test varies by state and by the law at issue.

**Does a signed contractor agreement make someone a contractor?**

No. It documents your intent, but agencies and courts look at the real working relationship.

**Can AI decide how to classify a worker?**

AI can explain the factors, but the answer depends on your state, facts, and applicable tests, so confirm with a professional.

**What should a contractor agreement include?**

Scope, deliverables, payment, ownership of work, confidentiality, and termination, among other terms.
