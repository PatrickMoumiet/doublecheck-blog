*Selling a company is a one-time event with real legal consequences. Here is how the process works and what to prepare.*

**Key takeaways:**

- The process runs from preparation to marketing, a letter of intent, buyer diligence, and closing.
- Prepare cap table, corporate records, IP assignments, key contracts, and employee agreements early.
- Price is only one term; reps and warranties, indemnity, escrow, and earn-outs decide what you keep.
- Use experienced M&A counsel and keep deal information out of public AI tools.

The best time to prepare for a sale is before you have a buyer.

![The sell-side process](/images/sell-side-ma-legal-checklist/process.webp)


## 1. Selling your company: the shape of the process

If you are considering selling your company, the legal work is a long process with several distinct phases. This article is general information, and every deal differs.

![The sell-side process: prepare, market, sign a letter of intent, buyer diligence, then sign and close](/images/sell-side-ma-legal-checklist/process.webp)

Most of the value you protect comes from preparing early and from the terms you agree, not from the final signing.

## 2. Prepare early

![Seven things to prepare early: clean cap table, corporate records, signed IP assignments, key customer contracts, employee agreements, financial records, and experienced counsel](/images/sell-side-ma-legal-checklist/prepare.webp)

Buyers will run due diligence similar to what funding investors do. Our [Series A legal due diligence checklist](/blog/series-a-legal-due-diligence-checklist/) lists the areas, and the same housekeeping helps in a sale. Fix gaps in IP assignments and records first; see [confidentiality and IP assignment agreements](/blog/confidentiality-ip-assignment-agreements-startups/).

## 3. The letter of intent

A letter of intent (LOI) usually sets the headline terms before detailed documents. Some provisions, such as exclusivity, may bind you even if the rest is non-binding, so read the LOI carefully and have counsel look at it before you sign.

## 4. Terms that matter most

![Terms that matter most: price, structure, reps and warranties, indemnity, escrow or holdback, and earn-out](/images/sell-side-ma-legal-checklist/key-terms.webp)

- **Price** and the form it takes (cash, stock, or both).
- **Structure:** stock sale or asset sale.
- **Representations and warranties:** your promises about the business.
- **Indemnity:** who pays if something turns out to be wrong.
- **Escrow or holdback:** money held back after closing.
- **Earn-out:** payments depending on future performance.

Price is only one number. The risk-allocation terms decide how much of it you actually keep.

## 5. Use AI carefully

AI can explain terms and prepare questions. In a sale, keep confidential deal information out of public AI tools unless you have checked the tool's terms; see [Heppner and AI chats](/blog/ai-chats-not-privileged-heppner/) and our [AI policy template](/blog/ai-policy-template-small-business/).

## 6. Get experienced counsel

A sale is a one-time, high-stakes transaction. Experienced M&A counsel is usually worth the cost. See [how to choose a good lawyer](/blog/how-to-choose-good-lawyer/).

## FAQ

**What is a sell-side M&A process?**

The process of a company's owners selling the business: preparing, marketing, agreeing a letter of intent, buyer diligence, and closing.

**What is a letter of intent?**

A document that usually sets headline deal terms before detailed agreements, sometimes with binding provisions such as exclusivity.

**What are representations and warranties?**

Promises the seller makes about the business, which can create liability if they turn out to be wrong.

**Do I need a lawyer to sell my company?**

Experienced M&A counsel is generally worth considering given the stakes.
