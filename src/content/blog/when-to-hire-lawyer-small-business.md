*You do not need a lawyer for everything, but some moments are worth it. Here is how to tell the difference.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Routine, low-stakes tasks can often start with research and AI help.
- Call a lawyer for major contracts, investors, sensitive hiring or firing, legal threats, deals, brand protection, and risky launches.
- The more money, equity, reputation, or deadline pressure, the more value in review.
- Start with AI, verify, then bring a lawyer in for what matters.

The cost of a lawyer is real, and so is the cost of a mistake. The skill is knowing when to spend.

## 1. You do not need a lawyer for everything

Many routine questions can start with research, templates, and AI help. The trick is knowing when the stakes rise enough that professional review is worth it.

## 2. Seven moments to call a lawyer

![Seven moments to call a lawyer: signing a major contract, taking on investors, hiring or firing, receiving a legal threat, selling or buying, protecting your brand, launching a risky product](/images/when-to-hire-lawyer-small-business/signals.webp)

1. Signing a major contract.
2. Taking on investors or giving out equity.
3. Hiring or firing, especially in a sensitive situation.
4. Receiving a legal threat or demand letter.
5. Buying or selling a business or major assets.
6. Protecting your brand or intellectual property.
7. Launching a product with real legal risk.

And an eighth rule: when something just feels off, ask.

## 3. Handle it yourself or get help?

![Handle it yourself or get help: low stakes work like internal templates and preparing questions can start with you, while large money, disputes, and regulated issues call for professional help](/images/when-to-hire-lawyer-small-business/diy-vs-lawyer.webp)

A rough test: the more money, equity, reputation, or deadline pressure involved, the more value there is in professional review.

## 4. A workflow that saves money

![Start with AI, finish with a lawyer: understand, organize, draft, verify, then review what matters](/images/when-to-hire-lawyer-small-business/ai-then-lawyer.webp)

Use AI to understand the issue, organize your facts and documents, and prepare a first draft. Verify what you can yourself. Then bring a lawyer in for the pieces that matter. See [saving money on legal work with AI](/blog/save-money-legal-work-with-ai/) and [how to choose a good lawyer](/blog/how-to-choose-good-lawyer/).

## 5. What to bring

A short summary, the key documents, your deadline, your goal, and your questions. If you used AI, say so and share what it produced. See [how to get a lawyer referral](/blog/how-to-get-lawyer-referral/).

## FAQ

**When does a small business need a lawyer?**

Common moments include major contracts, taking investors, sensitive employment actions, legal threats, buying or selling, protecting IP, and launching risky products.

**Can AI replace a lawyer for my business?**

AI can help you understand and prepare, but it does not replace professional judgment for high-stakes matters.

**How do I save money on legal work?**

Prepare your facts, documents, and questions in advance, use AI for first drafts and understanding, and ask the lawyer to review the parts that matter most.

**What should I tell a lawyer if I used AI?**

Say so and share the output. It can speed up the work and the lawyer can check it.
