*When a customer sends back your contract full of changes, a simple process keeps you in control.*

**Key takeaways:**

- Sort changes into cosmetic, commercial, and risk groups.
- Decide your must-haves before you respond.
- Check payment, renewal, termination, liability, indemnification, IP, data, service levels, and governing law.
- Use AI to compare, then verify and escalate high-stakes changes.

A redline can look intimidating. It is usually a handful of real issues in a lot of noise.

![Reviewing a customer's redlines](/images/review-customer-contract-redlines/redline-flow.webp)


## 1. When the customer sends changes

You sent your standard customer contract. The customer returns it covered in tracked changes. What now?

![Reviewing a customer's redlines: compare, sort cosmetic from real changes, decide your must-haves, respond, then sign after a final read](/images/review-customer-contract-redlines/redline-flow.webp)

Treat the redline as a list to sort, not an emergency.

## 2. Sort the changes into three groups

![Three kinds of changes: cosmetic wording changes, commercial changes about money and scope, and risk changes about who bears the loss](/images/review-customer-contract-redlines/change-types.webp)

- **Cosmetic:** wording, typos, formatting. Usually quick to accept.
- **Commercial:** price, payment timing, term, renewal, scope.
- **Risk:** liability, indemnification, IP, data, termination. These deserve the most attention.

## 3. Decide your must-haves first

Before replying, decide what you cannot give up, such as a liability cap, payment terms, or ownership of your product. Everything else becomes negotiable. A short internal list keeps you consistent.

## 4. Nine clauses to check

![Nine clauses to check in redlines: payment terms, term and renewal, termination, liability cap, indemnification, IP ownership, data and privacy, service levels, governing law](/images/review-customer-contract-redlines/nine-clauses.webp)

Check each of these in the customer's version against yours. Pay special attention to anything that changed liability, indemnity, or IP. For data terms see our [AI vendor contract checklist](/blog/ai-vendor-contract-checklist/).

## 5. Use AI to compare, then verify

AI can summarize what changed and group changes by type. It can miss a changed definition or a change buried in an exhibit, so confirm against the document. See [AI contract review for small business](/blog/ai-contract-review-small-business/) and [contract red flags](/blog/contract-red-flags-small-business/).

## 6. When to bring in a lawyer

Large contract value, unusual liability or IP changes, or terms that would change how you operate are good reasons. See [when to hire a lawyer](/blog/when-to-hire-lawyer-small-business/).

## FAQ

**How do I review redlines from a customer?**

Compare the versions, sort changes into cosmetic, commercial, and risk, decide your must-haves, and respond with accept or counter.

**Which clauses matter most in redlines?**

Liability, indemnification, IP ownership, data, termination, and payment terms.

**Can AI compare contract versions?**

It can summarize changes, but verify against the documents because it can miss subtle edits.

**When should a lawyer review a redline?**

For large contracts, unusual liability or IP changes, or terms that change how you operate.
