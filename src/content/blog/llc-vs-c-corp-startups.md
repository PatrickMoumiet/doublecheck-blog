*LLC or C-corp is one of the first big decisions founders make. Here is a simple way to think about it.*

**Key takeaways:**

- An LLC is flexible and run through an operating agreement; a C-corp has shareholders, a board, and officers.
- Founders planning venture funding are often advised to use a C-corp, commonly in Delaware.
- Ask about funding, owners, decisions, taxes, state, and equity before choosing.
- Use AI to prepare questions, and a tax professional and lawyer to decide.

There is no universally correct entity type. The right answer depends on your plans.

![LLC versus C-corp at a glance](/images/llc-vs-c-corp-startups/side-by-side.webp)


## 1. The basic difference

An **LLC** (limited liability company) and a **C-corporation** are both legal entities that can separate your personal assets from the business in many situations. They differ in structure, formalities, ownership, and how they are taxed and funded.

![LLC versus C-corp at a glance: an LLC has members and an operating agreement with flexible structure, while a C-corp has shareholders, a board and officers, and is common for venture funding](/images/llc-vs-c-corp-startups/side-by-side.webp)

- An LLC is owned by **members** and governed by an **operating agreement**. It is often simpler and more flexible.
- A C-corp is owned by **shareholders** and run through a **board and officers**. It is the structure many venture investors expect.

## 2. Six questions to ask first

Do not start with "Which is better?" Start with your situation:

![Six questions to ask first: will you raise venture money, how many owners, who makes decisions, how will you pay taxes, which state, will you issue equity](/images/llc-vs-c-corp-startups/six-questions.webp)

Founders planning to raise venture money are often advised to use a C-corp, commonly formed in Delaware. A small service business with one or two owners may prefer an LLC. Taxes are a major part of the decision, so ask a tax professional about your numbers.

## 3. Ownership and decisions

Whatever you choose, write down who owns what and who decides what. For an LLC that is the operating agreement; see our guide on [LLC operating agreements and AI](/blog/llc-operating-agreement-with-ai/). For co-founders, see the [co-founder agreement guide](/blog/co-founder-agreement-startups/).

## 4. What changes later

Founders often change structure when they raise money or add owners. Converting can be done, but it can involve filings, tax questions, and contract updates. It is cheaper to think about your likely path early.

## 5. Where AI helps and where it stops

AI is a good thinking partner: ask it to explain the terms, compare your scenarios, and list questions for your accountant and lawyer.

![Where AI helps and where it stops: AI helps prepare the conversation, while professionals decide tax consequences, investor expectations, and state-specific rules](/images/llc-vs-c-corp-startups/ai-help.webp)

## FAQ

**Is an LLC or C-corp better for a startup?**

It depends. Startups planning venture funding often choose a C-corp, while smaller owner-run businesses often prefer an LLC. Get tax and legal input.

**Can I switch from an LLC to a C-corp later?**

Yes, conversions are possible but can involve filings, tax questions, and contract updates.

**Can AI choose my entity type?**

AI can explain options and help prepare questions, but tax consequences and investor expectations should be confirmed with professionals.

**Do I need an operating agreement?**

If you form an LLC, a written operating agreement is strongly advisable so ownership and decision rules are clear.
