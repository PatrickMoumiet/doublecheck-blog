*Many small businesses were told to file a BOI report. In August 2026 the rule changed for U.S. companies.*

**Key takeaways:**

- FinCEN issued a final rule on August 11, 2026, effective August 14, exempting domestic companies and U.S. persons from BOI reporting.
- Foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S. must still report their non-U.S. owners and applicants.
- Old advice and old AI answers may still tell you to file.
- Keep your ownership records anyway, and watch for future rule changes.

If you have been getting emails about filing a BOI report, this is the update you need.

![What changed for U.S. companies](/images/beneficial-ownership-report-boi-2026/what-changed.webp)


## 1. What changed

For several years, many small companies were told they had to file a beneficial ownership information (BOI) report with FinCEN under the Corporate Transparency Act. That has changed.

According to a law-firm summary, on **August 11, 2026**, FinCEN issued a final rule exempting domestic companies and U.S. persons from BOI reporting. The rule took effect **August 14, 2026**, and expands an interim final rule Treasury issued in March 2025.

![What changed for U.S. companies: reports were required before, FinCEN's August 2026 final rule exempts U.S. companies, and foreign companies registered here still report](/images/beneficial-ownership-report-boi-2026/what-changed.webp)

In practical terms, a U.S. company no longer has to file a BOI report.

## 2. What the final rule covers

As summarized:

- U.S. companies no longer have to file BOI reports.
- Reporting companies do not need to report BOI for U.S. person beneficial owners.
- U.S. persons who previously obtained a FinCEN ID no longer have to update or correct it.
- FinCEN says it will delete previously reported BOI of U.S. persons and domestic companies from its database.

## 3. Who still has to file

Reporting still applies to **foreign companies registered to do business in the United States**, covering their non-U.S. beneficial owners and non-U.S. company applicants.

![Who still files a BOI report: U.S. companies are exempt, while foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S. still report their non-U.S. owners and applicants](/images/beneficial-ownership-report-boi-2026/who-files.webp)

If you run a U.S. LLC or corporation, you are generally outside the requirement. If your company is formed abroad but registered here, check the current rule carefully.

## 5. What to do now

If you formed or manage a company:

![What to do now: confirm your company type, read the rule, keep your records, watch for changes, and get advice if your case is unusual](/images/beneficial-ownership-report-boi-2026/do-now.webp)

- Confirm whether your company is domestic or a foreign company registered here.
- Read the final rule or a reliable summary rather than relying on old emails.
- Keep your formation and ownership records. They are still useful for banking, investors, and your own governance.
- Remember the rule could change again through future rulemaking.

## FAQ

**Do U.S. companies still have to file BOI reports?**

Under FinCEN's August 2026 final rule, domestic companies and U.S. persons are exempt from BOI reporting.

**Who still has to file a BOI report?**

Foreign companies registered to do business in the United States must continue reporting their non-U.S. beneficial owners and non-U.S. company applicants.

**What happens to BOI I already filed?**

FinCEN has said it will delete previously reported BOI of U.S. persons and domestic companies from its database.

**Could the rule change again?**

Yes. Commentators note a future administration could reinstate reporting for U.S. companies through new rulemaking, so check current sources.
