Business Name Check: Entity Name, Trademark, and Domain Are Not the Same

Written byPatrick Moumiet

  • 3. Similar names

Choosing a business name involves more than checking whether the domain is free. Here is a simple checklist.

Key takeaways:

  • Your entity name, trademark, and domain name are three different things.
  • Search trademark records, state business names, domains, social handles, and the web.
  • Trademark problems are about likelihood of confusion, not only exact matches.
  • Use AI to brainstorm and build a checklist, not to declare a name clear.

A name can be available on the state registry and still cause trouble. Run the checks before you print anything.

1. Three different things people mix up

When you pick a business name, you are dealing with several separate systems:

  • A business or entity name is registered with the state when you form your company.
  • A trademark relates to how a name or logo identifies your goods or services in the marketplace.
  • A domain name is your web address, registered through a registrar.

Having one does not automatically give you the others. A state accepting your LLC name does not mean the name is safe from a trademark conflict.

2. Seven checks before you pick a name

Search official trademark records, your state’s business name database, the domain, and social handles. Search the web for similar names, especially in your industry. Then think about whether you want to register the mark.

3. Similar is the problem, not only identical

Trademark conflicts are usually about likelihood of confusion, not exact matches. A name that is close to an existing brand in a related field can be a problem even if it is not identical. That judgment call is where professional advice is most useful.

4. Using AI for the search

AI can brainstorm names, produce lists of similar-sounding words to check, and build a search checklist. It cannot tell you whether a name is clear, and it may confidently say a name is “available” without checking any record. Treat that as a prompt to search, not an answer.

5. If you receive a trademark complaint

If someone claims your name infringes theirs, do not panic or admit anything. See our guide on responding to a cease-and-desist letter. For new companies, also see incorporating in the U.S..

FAQ

Is a registered LLC name the same as a trademark?

No. State registration of an entity name is separate from trademark rights in how a name is used in the marketplace.

Does owning the domain give me trademark rights?

No. A domain name is a web address and does not by itself create trademark rights.

Can AI tell me if a business name is available?

AI can help you build a search checklist, but you need to check the actual official records and assess similarity yourself or with professional help.

What if my name is close to another brand?

Closeness in a related field can create risk even without an exact match. Consider professional advice before investing in the name.
