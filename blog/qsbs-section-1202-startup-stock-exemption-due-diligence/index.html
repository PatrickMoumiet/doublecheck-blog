A definitive legal and tax diligence guide to Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) under Internal Revenue Code Section 1202, $10M capital gains exclusions, holding requirements, and AI diligence as of November 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Key takeaways:

  • IRC Section 1202 (QSBS) allows eligible founders, angel investors, and venture funds to exclude up to 100% of federal capital gains (up to $10,000,000 or 10x cash basis) on qualifying startup stock.
  • The startup must be an active domestic C corporation with aggregate gross assets under $50,000,000 at the time of stock issuance.
  • To claim the exemption, equity must be held for a minimum of five consecutive years from the date of original issuance.
  • Inadvertent corporate actions—especially share redemptions and stock buybacks from departing founders—can retroactively taint and destroy QSBS eligibility for entire investment rounds.
  • Institutional venture investors routinely demand affirmative QSBS covenants in Series A term sheets, requiring automated AI audits of historical equity records to substantiate eligibility.

1. The quick answer

Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) under Section 1202 of the Internal Revenue Code is widely considered the single most lucrative tax shelter in startup venture finance. Under current federal tax law, non-corporate shareholders who acquire original-issue stock in an early-stage C corporation and hold it for more than five years can exclude up to $10,000,000 (or 10 times their investment basis, whichever is greater) from federal capital gains taxes.

At a 20% federal capital gains rate plus the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT), QSBS saves founders and early investors up to $2,380,000 in federal taxes per $10 million in gain. Consequently, incoming Series A venture investors and angels treat QSBS verification as a critical legal due diligence item. Failing to properly document and protect QSBS eligibility can trigger deal renegotiations and indemnification claims.

2. The Section 1202 QSBS tax incentive framework

Section 1202 was enacted by Congress to encourage long-term risk investment in innovative startups. Depending on when stock was acquired, the federal gain exclusion percentage varies:

  • Stock Acquired on or after September 28, 2010: 100% federal capital gains exclusion (zero AMT preference and zero NIIT).
  • Stock Acquired between February 18, 2009 and September 27, 2010: 75% gain exclusion.
  • Stock Acquired before February 18, 2009: 50% gain exclusion.

For modern technology and AI startups formed today, all newly issued stock falls under the 100% exclusion regime, provided all statutory conditions are satisfied.

3. The five core statutory requirements for QSBS

To qualify for Section 1202 treatment, five strict legal and operational tests must be met continuously:

1. Domestic C Corporation Test

The company must be organized as a domestic US C corporation throughout the holding period. S corporations, LLCs, and foreign entities do not qualify. (If an LLC converts to a C corp, the QSBS holding clock starts on the conversion date).

2. Original Issuance Test

Stock must be acquired directly from the company at original issuance in exchange for money, property (excluding other stock), or services provided to the company. Secondary purchases from existing shareholders do not qualify.

3. $50 Million Gross Assets Test

At all times before and immediately after the stock issuance, the corporation’s aggregate gross assets (cash plus the adjusted tax basis of all corporate property) cannot exceed $50,000,000. Subsequent growth above $50M does not disqualify existing shares.

4. Active Business Requirement (80% Test)

At least 80% of the corporation’s assets must be actively used in the conduct of one or more qualified trades or businesses.

  • Disqualified Sectors: Professional services (health, law, engineering, accounting), banking, insurance, financing, leasing, farming, mining, and hospitality. Software, SaaS, biotech, hardware, and AI development are core qualified businesses.

5. Five-Year Holding Period

Shareholders must hold the stock for at least five full years before selling. If an acquisition occurs before five years, founders may roll over gains tax-free into another QSBS company under IRC Section 1045 within 60 days.

4. The redemption trap: How stock buybacks spoil QSBS

The most dangerous pitfall in startup corporate governance is the anti-evasion redemption rule under Section 1202(c)(3). Designed to prevent companies from disguising tax-free dividend distributions as stock sales, this rule can inadvertently disqualify all shares issued in a venture round:

  1. Related Shareholder Redemptions (4-Year Window): If the company repurchases any stock from the taxpayer or a related party within two years before or two years after stock is issued, the stock is disqualified from QSBS.
  2. Significant Redemptions (2-Year Window): If the company repurchases stock from any shareholder exceeding 2% of total corporate equity within one year before or one year after stock issuance, all stock issued in that round loses QSBS status.

Common Scenario: A co-founder leaves the company, and the startup repurchases their 500,000 unvested shares for $50,000 right before closing a $5M Series A round. Unless structured carefully under specific regulatory safe harbors (e.g., termination of employment), this buyback can wipe out QSBS tax benefits for all incoming Series A investors.

5. QSBS due diligence representations in Series A

Because QSBS delivers immense tax savings to venture fund General Partners and angel investors, standard NVCA financing agreements feature explicit covenants:

  • Company QSBS Representation: Certifying that the company has never exceeded the $50M asset test, has maintained active business operations, and has not executed disqualifying share redemptions.
  • Reporting Covenants: Committing to provide documentation and annual certification letters to investors upon request to support Section 1202 tax filings.

FAQ

What is Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) under Section 1202?

Section 1202 of the Internal Revenue Code allows founders and investors to exclude up to 100% of capital gains (up to $10 million or 10 times basis) from federal income tax upon selling qualifying C-corp stock held for at least five years.

What is the $50 million gross assets test for QSBS?

The aggregate gross assets of the corporation (cash plus adjusted tax basis of property) cannot exceed $50 million immediately before and immediately after the stock is issued.

Can an LLC qualify for QSBS tax benefits?

No. Stock must be issued by a domestic C corporation; however, an LLC can convert to a C corp, with QSBS holding periods beginning on the date of corporate conversion.

How do corporate share redemptions destroy QSBS eligibility?

If the company repurchases stock from the founder or another shareholder within two years before or after stock issuance (or significant redemptions within one year), it can disqualify QSBS status for all shares issued during that window.
